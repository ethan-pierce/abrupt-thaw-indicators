\section{Introduction}

\begin{enumerate}
	\item[P1a.] Abrupt permafrost thaw affects a small subset of the landscape, but represents a large amount of carbon.
	\item[P1b.] Abrupt thaw's contribution to permafrost-carbon feedbacks depends on its prevalence and spatial pattern.
	\item[P1c.] Abrupt thaw represents an immediate threat to human infrastructure, and does so in disproportionately fragile communities.

	\item[P2a.] Predicting and adapting to risk from abrupt thaw thus depends on our ability to map it across the landscape.
	\item[P2b.] Current permafrost maps characterize the substrate, including extent and zonation (Brown 1997), ice content (Jorgensen 2008), and probability (Obu 2019).
	\item[P2b.] Occurence describes which specific features are present, and has been mapped for certain landforms, including thermokarst lakes (Wang 2023) and thaw slumps (Nitze 2025).
	\item[P2c.] Stage describes how far along thaw has progressed, and has been mapped recently (Zhang 2025).
	\item[P2d.] These existing products describe where permafrost is, what features are present, and how much thaw has progressed, but they do not identify the mode of thaw.

	\item[P3a.] Abrupt thaw is distinct from gradual thaw in its pace, impact on ecosystems, and surface expression (Webb 2025).
	\item[P3b.] Abrupt thaw remains poorly mapped, with an uncertain spatial extent, often reliant on expert identification in the field (Webb 2026).
	\item[P3c.] There are no continuous, state-wide assessments of susceptibility to abrupt thaw.

	\item[P4a.] Here, we train a machine learning model to distinguish abrupt from non-abrupt thaw across Alaska's permafrost domain.
	\item[P4b.] Our model is trained on the Alaska Thaw Database, which includes 19,000 thaw sites, categorized by expert knowledge into abrupt or non-abrupt thaw.
	\item[P4c.] We assemble 70 geospatial features from statewide data products, satellite remote sensing, and model reanalysis.
	\item[P4d.] The resulting map represents a continuous index of how closely features match known abrupt thaw sites.
	\item[P4e.] We then analyze Shapley values to interpret which geospatial features best distinguish abrupt from non-abrupt thaw.
	\item[P4f.] Our results give the first continuous, statewide characterization of thaw mode across Alaska, along with the key geospatial indicators associate with abrupt thaw.
\end{enumerate}
