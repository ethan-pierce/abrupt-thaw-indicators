# Abrupt thaw and the permafrost–carbon feedback: what this study newly supports

## Bottom line

The defensible novelty is a **spatial conditioning layer for thaw mode**, not a new carbon-budget estimate. Earlier work established that abrupt thaw can mobilize deep, old carbon quickly and can contribute to the permafrost–carbon feedback out of proportion to its area, but it represented abrupt thaw through aggregate inventories, prescribed areal fractions, or particular landforms. This study instead learns the contrast between observed abrupt and non-abrupt sites across multiple landform types and maps, at nominal ~1 km resolution across Alaska, the prior-free log evidence that a cell's environmental setting resembles one mode more than the other. Its paired Area of Applicability (AoA) mask identifies environmental combinations not represented in the training data.

That result fills a spatial gap between carbon-feedback models and landform maps: within Alaska and within the AoA, it shows **where the abrupt pathway is relatively favored over the non-abrupt pathway, and which mapped environmental indicators support that distinction**. It can motivate spatially differentiated monitoring and future carbon-model parameterization. It does **not** estimate carbon stocks or fluxes, methane fraction, thaw timing, current or future occurrence probability, or a revised magnitude of the permafrost–carbon feedback.

## Defensible claim ladder

1. **Strongest claim:** “To our knowledge, this is the first continuous, observation-trained map of thaw mode across Alaska.” Keep “to our knowledge,” and immediately define mode as relative evidence for abrupt versus non-abrupt settings—not occurrence.
2. **Carbon-feedback relevance:** Prior carbon studies showed why mode matters; this study adds spatial information about where the landscape resembles the abrupt pathway. It therefore offers an empirical basis for **future** spatial conditioning or evaluation of abrupt-thaw representation in carbon models.
3. **Specific advance:** The log-evidence transformation removes the training sample's raw class prevalence, important because 94.3% of retained labels are abrupt even though that is not landscape prevalence. Spatial cross-validation tests geographic transfer, and the AoA flags covariate-space extrapolation ([Methods](../manuscript/sections/methods.tex); [Results](../manuscript/sections/results.tex)).
4. **New result that may be stated carefully:** Within the AoA, 24.7% of the mapped permafrost domain has positive log evidence (about 264,000 km²), while abrupt-favoring terrain is spatially concentrated in northern and western tundra regions. This is the area above a neutral evidence threshold, **not** an estimate that 24.7% is thawing abruptly or will do so.
5. **Mechanistic insight:** The map shows spatially varying indicator combinations rather than a single statewide control; topography and temperature lead overall, with low-lying and steeper settings both favoring abrupt labels. SHAP attribution describes model discrimination, not causation.

## Claims to avoid

- “We estimate Alaska's abrupt-thaw carbon emissions” or “we revise the magnitude of the permafrost–carbon feedback.” No carbon-stock, flux, decomposition, or radiative-forcing model is included.
- “We predict where/when abrupt thaw will occur.” The inputs and labels are static and cannot distinguish already-thawed, actively thawing, and future-thaw sites.
- “24.7% of Alaska is susceptible to/will undergo abrupt thaw.” The manuscript reports the fraction with positive **relative log evidence** inside its AoA.
- “Our value validates/confirms the ~20% estimate.” The quantities differ in geography, definition, model target, and threshold. Similar magnitudes are a comparison, not validation.
- “The AoA is a reliability or accuracy bound.” Following [Meyer and Pebesma (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.13650), an AoA identifies predictor space represented by training data; in this manuscript it does not establish a cell-wise error rate or prove accuracy.
- “Abrupt thaw always releases more methane.” Walter Anthony et al. support strong methane forcing for thermokarst lakes, and Turetsky et al. distinguish lake/wetland/hillslope trajectories. Prefer: abrupt thaw can rapidly mobilize deeper carbon, while inundated lake and wetland pathways can increase methane's contribution.
- “Independent of landform.” The target spans many landforms and is not restricted to one named feature, but the training labels arise from landform-rich observations and are heavily weighted toward lowland aquatic settings. “Across multiple abrupt-thaw expressions” is safer.
- “~1 km² cells” as an exact area. The grid is nominally 1 km on 0.00898° geographic cells, so true cell area varies with latitude ([Data](../manuscript/sections/data.tex)).

## Suggested Discussion paragraph

Previous syntheses established that abrupt thaw can mobilize deep, old permafrost carbon rapidly and contribute to the permafrost–carbon feedback out of proportion to its area, but those estimates represented abrupt thaw through aggregate inventories or individual landform classes, including thermokarst lakes, wetlands, and hillslope failures \citep{olefeldt2016circumpolar, walter201821st, turetsky2020carbon}. Our study supplies a different, spatially explicit piece of that problem: within the area of applicability, we map at nominal 1-km resolution whether environmental conditions more strongly resemble sites observed to undergo abrupt or non-abrupt thaw, across multiple abrupt-thaw expressions rather than for a single named landform. This log-evidence surface does not estimate carbon release, methane fraction, the timing or probability of thaw, or a revised feedback magnitude. Instead, it identifies where the abrupt pathway is relatively favored over the non-abrupt pathway and where that inference is supported by the environmental range of the training data, providing an empirical spatial framework for targeting carbon observations and, ultimately, conditioning abrupt-thaw representations in permafrost-carbon models.

## Shorter, more assertive alternative

Abrupt-thaw studies have shown that rapid access to deep carbon can amplify the permafrost–carbon feedback, but carbon inventories and landform-susceptibility maps have not resolved the broader abrupt-versus-non-abrupt pathway at landscape scale \citep{olefeldt2016circumpolar, walter201821st, turetsky2020carbon}. We provide that missing spatial layer for Alaska: a nominal 1-km, observation-trained log-evidence surface that contrasts the environmental settings of abrupt and non-abrupt thaw across multiple landform expressions, accompanied by an area-of-applicability mask. The product does not predict thaw timing or occurrence and cannot by itself estimate carbon flux or revise the feedback magnitude; rather, it shows where abrupt-thaw carbon processes are most defensibly prioritized for observation and future model representation.
